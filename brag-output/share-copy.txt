I drew a lucky cat, and then spent far too long teaching it to swing properly.

Tassel hangs a hand-drawn charm from your Mac's menu bar on a real little rope. Flick it and the cord bends. Pull it right down and let go, and it springs back and tumbles. None of that is animation — it is a rope simulation running behind the menu bar, so the charm behaves like an object instead of a GIF.

Every charm also asks for its own small ritual. The daruma is my favourite: you paint one eye when you make a wish, and the other when it comes true.

Seven charms so far, all drawn by hand. Four sizes. It remembers where you left it, and it stays out of your way.

Free, open source, macOS 14+, Apple Silicon and Intel. One line to install — it's in the comments.

If it makes you smile, a star on GitHub would mean a lot.

=== LINKEDIN: A REPLY, IF SOMEONE ASKS HOW IT WORKS ===
Only if asked.

The cord is 24 points solved with Verlet integration: distance constraints, a little bend stiffness, and a heavier node at the end so the charm throws the rope around rather than the other way round. Dragging pins that last node to the pointer, which is what makes the stretch and the recoil fall out for free. It is all in Sources/TasselCore/Rope.swift, about 500 lines.
