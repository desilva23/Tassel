Tassel hangs a hand-drawn lucky charm from your Mac's menu bar, on a little rope. No sync. No streaks. No notifications. No AI. It doesn't want your email address.

What it does is swing. Flick the cord and it bends. Pull the charm right down and let go, and it springs back and tumbles — that's a real rope simulation, 24 points of it, running quietly up there.

Every charm also asks for one small ritual. The daruma gets one eye painted when you make a wish, and the other when it comes true.

Everything else in my menu bar is asking me for something. This one just hangs there and sways.

Seven charms, all drawn by hand. Free, open source, macOS 14+. Install line's in the comments.

If there's a charm you'd hang in your menu bar, tell me — I'll draw the ones I can.

A star on GitHub would make my week.

Fair point — it is worth reading first, and it is short: raw.githubusercontent.com/desilva23/Tassel/main/install.sh

It finds the latest release, checks the download against the sha256 published in the repo's Homebrew cask, copies the app to Applications and clears the quarantine flag macOS puts on anything downloaded. The Homebrew cask does exactly the same things. If you would rather have neither, the DMG is on the releases page.


=== LINKEDIN: A REPLY, IF SOMEONE ASKS HOW IT WORKS ===
Only if asked.

The cord is 24 points solved with Verlet integration: distance constraints, a little bend stiffness, and a heavier node at the end so the charm throws the rope around rather than the other way round. Dragging pins that last node to the pointer, which is what makes the stretch and the recoil fall out for free. It is all in Sources/TasselCore/Rope.swift, about 500 lines.
